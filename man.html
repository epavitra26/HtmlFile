Networks

A Local Area Network (LAN) connects computers and devices within a limited area—such as a home, school, or office—so they can communicate and share resources like files, printers, and an internet connection. Most modern LANs use Ethernet cables, Wi-Fi, or both, and typically offer higher speeds and lower latency than wide area networks (WANs).

A Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) is [Highlight start] a computer network that connects multiple Local Area Networks (LANs) across a city or large metropolitan region, typically spanning 5 to 50 kilometers [Highlight end] . It sits between a LAN (single building or small area) and a Wide Area Network (WAN, covering countries or the globe) in size and scope.

• Infrastructure: Organizations typically own their internal LANs, while service providers (ISPs, telecom carriers) operate the long-distance links using fiber-optic cables, leased lines, cellular networks (4G/5G), or satellites. • Routing & Switching: Data is split into packets and sent across edge routers and carrier networks toward its destination.
